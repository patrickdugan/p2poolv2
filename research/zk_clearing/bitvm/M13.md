# M13: rising fees during an assert, and binding batchHeight to the chain

Run 2026-11-01 on Bitcoin Core v31.1.0 (regtest), on M9's anchored graph with the same BitVM
build, patch, proof and demo keys as [M2.md](M2.md) and [M9.md](M9.md). Regtest only: the keys
and the Groth16 setup are public.

## Headline

- **Fees rising during an assert.** The market went from 10 to 20 sat/vB after assert_init and
  its CPFP child were posted. The operator replaced the child by RBF and carried the rest of the
  chain at 20 sat/vB. A was confirmed at h_k + 8 with 3 blocks to spare, as at 2 and 10 sat/vB.
  Above MAX_FEE_RATE (10 sat/vB) the challenge no longer covers the assert: the operator spent
  8,481,820 sats on it and the challenge paid 4,250,000.
- **batchHeight bound from both sides.**
  - *From below:* the kickoff's lock time is the statement's batchHeight.
  - *From above:* the kickoff also spends a deadline connector that anyone may sweep 4 blocks
    later.
  - *For buyers:* each escrow refunds through CLTV at an absolute expiry, the expiry the buyer
    signs in M4.
  - On regtest, the kickoff was refused before batchHeight and after the sweep. Four scenarios
    behave as in M9.

## 1. Fees rising during an assert

`run-m13.sh <work> rise` starts a node with `-blockmintxfee` at 10 sat/vB and runs `challenged`
with `ZKC_RISE_TO=20`. After assert_init's child is posted, the scenario:

1. restarts the node with `-blockmintxfee` at 20 sat/vB through a script that `run-m13.sh`
   writes. The node saves its mempool at shutdown and loads it at start.
2. checks the template;
3. lets the operator replace its child (same anchor, same coin, a higher fee: RBF);
4. carries every later transaction at 20 sat/vB.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| assert_init (1,917 vB) and its child, after the restart | both still in the mempool; out of the template at 20 sat/vB |
| The operator's replacement child (169 vB, same inputs) | accepted; the old child is evicted |
| assert_init with the replacement | in the template; mined at h_k + 6 |
| commit[0..4], each with a child at 20 sat/vB | each out of the template alone and in it with its child; all mined at h_k + 7 |
| assert_final with a child | mined at h_k + 8, 3 blocks before the timeout opens |
| payout_asserted with a child | mined at h_A + 6; outputs equal the template |

**What the rise costs the operator.** The challenge pays the assert chain's cost at
MAX_FEE_RATE = 10 sat/vB, 4,250,000 sats (M9 section 4). At 20 sat/vB the chain cost the operator:

- 846,938 sats of presigned fees;
- 7,634,882 sats in children.

That is 8,481,820 sats in all, so the operator ended 4,231,820 sats short on the assert. It still
asserted in time, and it kept its bond. That is M9's reading of MAX_FEE_RATE: above it an honest
operator who is challenged loses money but not the bond. The kickoff's child (3,610 sats at
10 sat/vB) and payout_asserted's (15,836 at 20 sat/vB) are costs on either path.

RBF on a keyed anchor needs nothing special: only the operator can spend the anchor, so its own
children are the only ones that conflict.

## 2. Binding batchHeight to the chain

**The problem.** M4 and M11 check `batchHeight <= orderExpiry` and `batchHeight <= escrowExpiry`
inside the proof. The operator supplies `batchHeight`.

- A **lower** bound on chain alone protects nothing. An operator who names a later height than
  the real one only makes the expiry checks stricter.
- What an expiry needs is an **upper** bound: an old statement must not settle long after its
  batchHeight.
- Bitcoin has no rule that makes a transaction invalid after a height. A transaction can only be
  invalidated by spending one of its inputs first.

**The construction** (`ZKC_BIND_HEIGHT=1`, [`m2/src/graph.rs`](m2/src/graph.rs)):

| Part | Script | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Kickoff lock time | `nLockTime = batchHeight` | the graph cannot start before batchHeight + 1 |
| Deadline connector D, funded with the bond, spent by the kickoff | P2TR, internal key = operator; one leaf `<batchHeight + 4> OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY` | from block batchHeight + 5, anyone can spend D with an empty witness and keep its value (10,000 sats here). Once D is spent, the kickoff can never confirm |
| Escrow refund leaf | `<escrowExpiry> OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY OP_DROP <buyer> OP_CHECKSIG` (M2 had `T_refund OP_CSV`) | the buyer can leave at the height it signed, whatever the operator does |

So the kickoff can confirm from block batchHeight + 1. From block batchHeight + 5 it races the
sweep of D, and once D is swept it never confirms. Everything after the kickoff runs on the
graph's own clocks:

- the unchallenged payout opens T_challenge after the kickoff and has to beat the timeout at
  T_assert;
- a challenged batch's payout opens T_disprove after A, which comes before T_assert;
- every escrow's CLTV refund ends both.

**What makes the sweep happen.** D's value goes to whoever spends it, including a miner who
includes its own sweep. A late kickoff therefore has to outbid D's value in fees to be mined
instead. With D at 10,000 sats that is cheap to overcome. A deployment sizes D against what a late
settlement would be worth to an operator. The deadline then holds as long as someone, a miner or
any watcher, wants the 10,000 sats more than the operator wants the kickoff.

**Runs** (`run-m13.sh <work> bound`; funding at h_e, batchHeight = h_e + 2, deadline = h_e + 6,
escrow expiry = h_e + 29):

| Scenario | What happened | vbytes mined |
|---|---|---|
| unchallenged | the kickoff refused at tip h_e (`non-final`), mined at h_e + 3 = batchHeight + 1; the payout at h_k + 6 | 998 (M9: 940; the kickoff gains the 58 vB input of D) |
| silent | the same kickoff; challenged; the timeout slashes at h_k + 12. Refunds refused at tip h_e + 28 (`non-final`) and mined at h_e + 30, the block M2's CSV refunds opened | 1,079 |
| stall | no kickoff; CLTV refunds as above; bond and D unspent | 390 |
| late | the operator does not kick off by the deadline. The sweep is refused at tip h_e + 5 (`non-final`) and mined at h_e + 7 by "anyone", who keeps 9,790 sats. The kickoff is then refused (`missing-inputs`); buyers refund; the bond is untouched | 495 |

**What this leaves to the circuit.** The deadline height has to come from the statement. Here it
is batchHeight + 4, fixed by the graph. For the sellers' expiries to bind:

- the circuit has to prove `batchHeight + 4 <= orderExpiry` for every sale, not only
  `batchHeight <= orderExpiry`;
- escrow refund leaves have to use the buyer-signed `escrowExpiry`.

Both are one-line changes to M4's and M11's range checks and to the graph. They are not made here.

**Seller protection.** A seller's order expiry now holds on chain if the circuit makes the
change above and someone sweeps D. Before M13 it held only if the committee refused stale
statements.

## Reproduce

M13 reuses M9's work dir (`/root/zkc-m9`, prepared as in M9.md):

```sh
BITVM_SRC=/root/zkc-m1/BitVM-c620dbf bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/build.sh /root/zkc-m9
BITCOIN_BIN=/root/opt/bitcoin-31.1/bin bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/run-m13.sh /root/zkc-m9 rise
BITCOIN_BIN=/root/opt/bitcoin-31.1/bin bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/run-m13.sh /root/zkc-m9 bound
```

[`results/m13/`](results/m13/) holds one trace per scenario (every txid, size, mempool verdict,
template probe, rejection and mined height) and the run times.
