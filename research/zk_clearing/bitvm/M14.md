# M14: BitVM on M11's signed-tree statement

Run 2026-11-01. Everything below was executed and measured unless marked *est.* The demo keys and
Groth16 setup are public, so **none of it is safe with real funds**; it is regtest only.

The question: does M3's BitVM settlement carry over to M11's circuit, which proves signed seller
orders and signed buyer escrow terms behind one hashed public input, and what does the larger
statement change on chain?

- **Machine:** AMD Ryzen AI 9 365, WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04 with a 19 GB VM, Rust 1.99.1. The same
  machine as [M3.md](M3.md). Other sessions' jobs ran in the VM during these runs (an M9 scenario
  during `prepare`), so times carry that noise; peak memory is the comparison to rely on.
- **Groth16:** `signed_tree_16x4` ([`signedtree/circuits/`](../signedtree/circuits/signed_tree_16x4.circom),
  `SignedClearingTree(16, 4, 4)`), set up with the insecure deterministic beacon on M6's power-20
  phase-1 file. The verifying key's sha256 is
  `ef1a22e36e7516d5afe131975e866575f113036e18a9a195148f3029bf4ad364`, the same as in
  [SIGNED_TREE.md](../signedtree/SIGNED_TREE.md).
- **Verifier:** BitVM `c620dbf` with [`patches/0001`](patches/0001-bitvm-18-public-inputs.patch)
  and [`patches/0002`](patches/0002-bitvm-1-public-input.patch), unchanged from M3 (`NUM_PUBS = 1`,
  `NUM_HASH = 363`).
- **Graph and node:** M1's graph (no kickoff or challenge), Bitcoin Core v31.1.0 on a private
  regtest node, RPC port 18783.

## Headline

**M11's proof settles through BitVM exactly as M3's did.** The five scenarios behave as in M3:
honest pays the template, tampered is disproved at the final pairing leaf, mismatch at the binding
leaf, plus-r at the first MSM leaf, and stall refunds the buyers.

**BitVM's side is M3's.** The verifying key has one public input, so the verifier has 364 disprove
leaves of 1,110,838,447 bytes. 359 of them are byte-identical to M3's; the other 5 (leaves 2–6)
embed the verifying key's `IC` points, and the sizes of leaves 2–5 move by −65 to +44 bytes.
The assert chain is 336,520 vB, against M3's 336,528.

**What grows is the payout, not the proof.** The circuit is 11.2 times larger (112,921 constraints
against 10,078) and proves in 5.8 s instead of 1.0 s (`run.sh`; the regtest batch takes 8.0 s), but the proof is 256 bytes with one public
signal either way. On chain the payout grows from 727 to 1,411 vB because the batch has 16 seller
outputs, 4 refunds and 4 escrows (M3: 3, 3 and 3).

## Result

| Scenario | What the operator asserts | Outcome on regtest |
|---|---|---|
| honest | h = statementHash of the proven statement S, and π | BitVM validation finds no disprovable chunk. The payout pays exactly the template (21 outputs plus the bond back), 6 blocks after A. Consensus rejects disproves through the final pairing leaf and through the binding leaf (each with the honest witness), and the payout one block early |
| tampered | h′ = H(S′) with π; the committee's template is S′, which hashes to h′ | BitVM leaf 363 (final pairing check) disproves in a 238,914 vB transaction; 9,326,972 sats slashed; the payout is rejected (`bad-txns-inputs-missingorspent`); buyers refund |
| mismatch | h and π, but the payout was presigned for S′ (template hash h′) | BitVM finds nothing (π is valid for h). The single binding leaf (364) disproves in a 3,134 vB standard transaction; buyers refund |
| plus-r | h written as h + r | BitVM MSM leaf 2 disproves (its `Fr::is_valid` range check, 633,473 vB); the binding leaf also opens |

S′ is M3's tampered statement with a smaller amount: 10,000 sats of refund moved from buyer
slot 1 (31,000 sats) to buyer slot 0. It conserves the total and builds a different template, but
no valid proof exists for it. (M3 moved 50,000; buyer slot 1 of this batch refunds 31,000.)

Disprove leaves are the same indices as M3's: 363 for the final pairing check, 364 for the binding
leaf, 2 for the first MSM chunk.

## Reproduce

Everything runs in WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04, in a native directory. These runs used `/root/zkc-m14` and
regtest RPC port 18783.

```sh
# 1. M11's circuit set up on M6's power-20 phase-1 file (signedtree/run_size.sh: every negative
#    check, the padding check), then the regtest batch (bitvm/batch_signed.py, signed by
#    signedtree/sign.js) proved with the same proving key, its statement recomputed with
#    statement_signed_tree.js, and batch/circuit.txt written.
PATH=/root/zkc-tools/node/bin:/root/zkc-tools/bin:$PATH PTAU=/root/zkc-m6/pot20_final.ptau \
  bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/prove-batch.sh /root/zkc-m14 signed_tree_16x4
# 2. BitVM c620dbf with patches 0001 and 0002, the workspace, and its tests.
ZKC_PUBS=1 BITVM_SRC=/root/zkc-m1/BitVM-c620dbf bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/build.sh /root/zkc-m14
# 3. Clean disprove scripts and assertions, timed.
/usr/bin/time -f "%e s, %M KiB" /root/zkc-m14/target/release/zkc-m1 prepare /root/zkc-m14
# 4. Conversion reports, a private bitcoind -regtest, every scenario.
ZKC_RPC_PORT=18783 BITCOIN_BIN=/root/opt/bitcoin-31.1/bin \
  bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/run-m1.sh /root/zkc-m14
# 5. Script-level checks without a node, including the x + r sweep (one signal).
/root/zkc-m14/target/release/zkc-m1 selftest /root/zkc-m14 --all-plus-r
```

`BITVM_SRC` points at a local clone of BitVM; without it `build.sh` clones from GitHub.
`PTAU` defaults to `/root/zkc-m6/pot20_final.ptau`.

The files in `vectors/` named `signed-*` are what step 1 wrote: `signed-batch-{input,opening,proof,
public,statement}.json` from `batch/`, `signed-verification_key.json` and `signed-example-*` from
`signed_tree_16x4/`, `signed-m11-16x4-statement.json` from its `statement.json`, and
`signed-m11-128x8-statement.json` from M11's 128×8 run (`statement_signed_tree.js` on its
`valid.json`). `chunked-hash-vectors.json` is `node chunked_vectors.js <node_modules>`.

**Selecting the statement.** `zkc-m1` reads the circuit's name from `<work>/batch/circuit.txt`,
which `prove-batch.sh` writes (`signed_tree_16x4`, `accounting16x4_hashed`,

`template::build_signed`; the accounting names, and a batch with no `circuit.txt` (M1's and M3's
batches), select M3's 18-value statement and `template::build_hashed`. A signed-tree statement
needs the build with `ZKC_PUBS=1`, and `zkc-m1` refuses it otherwise. `run-m1.sh` takes the
conversion example from `<work>/<circuit>` when `circuit.txt` names a directory there.

[`results/m14/`](results/m14/) holds what these runs wrote:
- the circuit's `run.sh` results (`groth16-signed-tree-results.txt`);
- the conversion reports;
- the disprove-leaf survey, and a comparison of the disprove scripts with M3's
  (`leaf-comparison-with-m3.txt`);

## 1. The circuit and the batch

`signed_tree_16x4` is M11's circuit at 16 sale slots and 4 buyer slots, unchanged. Its one public
signal is

```
statement     = [saleCommitment, escrowCommitment, batchHeight, totalPayout,
                 funding[0..3], feeBudget[0..3], refund[0..3], buyerId[0..3]]        (20 values)
statementHash = PoseidonChunked(statement)
```

`PoseidonChunked` hashes up to 12 values with one Poseidon, and longer lists as chunks of 12
whose digests are hashed together. For 20 values it is `Poseidon2(Poseidon12(v[0..12]),
Poseidon8(v[12..20]))`. Every call has at most 12 inputs, so light-poseidon computes it.

The setup reproduces M11's verifying key (sha256 above): same circuit, same phase-1 file, same
beacon. `run.sh` passes all its checks on it (10 invalid fixtures rejected, tampered and
`x + r` public signals rejected, forged witness rejected, re-split sale list proves a different
hash, padded batch satisfies the R1CS).

**The regtest batch** ([`batch_signed.py`](batch_signed.py)) is M11's deterministic 16 × 4 batch
with every slot active: 16 sales to 16 distinct sellers, 4 buyers, and sale `s` bought by buyer
`s mod 4`. One change: M11's refunds include 290 sats for buyer slot 2, under the 330-sat
P2TR dust limit, so the refunds are set to 38,000, 31,000, 12,000 and 17,000 and the funding
follows. It is signed with M11's demo keys (`sign.js`) and proved with the same proving key.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sales | 16, 1,884 to 90,377 sats, total 658,302 |
| Escrows | 225,435; 165,675; 184,341; 191,144 sats (766,595 in all) |
| Refunds / fee budgets | 38,000 / 31,000 / 12,000 / 17,000; 1,082 / 380 / 4,231 / 4,600 (10,293 in all) |
| `batchHeight` | 905,000 |
| `statementHash` | `17432278223305091840820969651034215976393192442233703969418213464535915499964` |
| Witness / prove | 2.99 s / 7.97 s (2.18 GiB) |

**The ids are labels the graph pays.** Every id the circuit signs is a field encoding of a label
(`field_id(kind, label)`, SHA-256 truncated to 253 bits), and the batch uses the labels the regtest
graph maps to keys:

| Signed id | Label | Where the graph uses it |
|---|---|---|
| `sellerPayoutId[s]` | `scale-payout:<s>` | the payout output to that payee's key-path script |
| `refundScriptId[b]` | `scale-refund:<b>` | the refund output |
| `escrowId[b]` | `scale-escrow:<b>:0` | named by the opening; the harness funds the escrow |

The opening (`batch/opening.json`) carries the labels per slot.

## 2. BitVM with M11's verifying key

M3's patch 0002 is unchanged: one public input, `NUM_HASH = 363`. BitVM's verifier depends on the
verifying key's shape, not on the circuit, and this key has the same shape as M3's.

**Disprove leaves** ([`results/m14/leaves.json`](results/m14/leaves.json),
[`leaf-comparison-with-m3.txt`](results/m14/leaf-comparison-with-m3.txt)):
- 364 leaves, 1,110,838,447 bytes in all, 80 fewer than M3's.
- Smallest 338,312 B, median 2,889,666 B, largest 3,948,062 B (leaf 357): the same as M3.
- Of the 364 disprove scripts, 359 are byte-identical to M3's: leaves 0–1 and 7–363.
- Leaves 2–6 differ. Leaves 2–5 are the four MSM chunks of the one public scalar; leaf 6 follows
  them. Sizes: 2,505,932 → 2,505,894, 2,588,567 → 2,588,502, 2,588,552 → 2,588,531 and
  2,588,542 → 2,588,586 bytes (partial scripts, M3 → M14); leaf 6 has the same size and different
  bytes.
- The two verifying keys differ only in `IC`. `vk_alpha_1`, `vk_beta_2`, `vk_gamma_2`,
  `vk_delta_2` and `vk_alphabeta_12` are equal: both setups use the same deterministic beacon
  (`5ea82d8c…`), and snarkjs sets gamma to the G2 generator. So the leaf differences come from
  the `IC` constants (the two points of `gamma_abc_g1`). This pair of keys cannot show whether
  other leaves would change under a verifying key with different alpha, beta or delta, which is
  what a real ceremony would give.

So the answer to whether BitVM's leaves changed: not in number, shape, tree depth or in the
sizes that matter (median, largest, count over the relay limit). A new circuit with one public
input changes five of the 364 scripts and nothing else, and `prepare` has to run again for it,
because the scripts are cached by verifying key.

| Step | M3 | M14 |
|---|---|---|
| Partial disprove scripts (depend on the verifying key only; cached) | 192 s | 126 s |
| Honest assertions (generated and checked by executing every leaf) | 230 s | 253 s |
| Tampered assertions | 260 s | 164 s |
| Clean `prepare` in all: time / peak RSS | 689 s / 5.95 GB | 539 s / 5.95 GB |
| Challenger check of an honest assertion (every leaf) | 222 s | 142 s |
| Challenger check in the tampered, mismatch and plus-r runs | 118–137 s; 3 s for plus-r | 133, 138 and 3 s |
| Peak RSS of each scenario that builds the graph | 3.25–3.27 GB | 3.26–3.27 GB |
| `selftest --all-plus-r` | 62 s, 2.9 GB | 7.9 s, 2.9 GB |

The self-test row is not a like-for-like pair: M14's ran with the disprove scripts already cached
by the scenario run, M3's generated them. Its checks are the same and all pass: the binding leaf
is shut on the honest hash and open on `h + r` and on H(S′), and `h + r` opens MSM leaf 2 with a
102-item witness that executes.

## 3. The payout template checks the preimage, the commitments and the scripts

[`m1/src/template.rs`](m1/src/template.rs) adds `PoseidonChunked` and `build_signed`.

**What changed from M3.**
1. `signed_statement_hash` computes `PoseidonChunked` with light-poseidon 0.3.0 (circom
   parameters, arkworks 0.5): up to 12 values per call, chunks of 12, and a second level for up to
   144 values (B up to 35). It refuses any value ≥ r, so `x + r` cannot stand in for `x`, and
   refuses 145 values, which the circuit cannot hash either.
2. `build_signed(statementHash, statement, input, opening)` checks, in order:
   1. `statementHash` is below r and the statement hashes to it;
   2. `saleCommitment` opens to the sale list and `escrowCommitment` to the escrow terms, both
      recomputed in Rust from the circuit input (`sale_commitment`, `escrow_commitment`: M6's
      Poseidon2 tree over Poseidon6 leaves, and `PoseidonChunked` over Poseidon5 leaves), and
      `buyerId[b]` is the sale list's;
   3. every active sale's `sellerPayoutId` is `field_id("seller_payout_script", payee)` for the
      payee label the opening gives, and every active buyer's `escrowId` and `refundScriptId` are
      the ids of its escrow and refund labels;
   4. M1's checks on the amounts: prices sum to `totalPayout`, outputs conserve the escrowed value,
      no output under the dust limit.
3. `Kind` (`Accounting` or `SignedTree { sales, buyers }`) carries the layout: statement length,
   the index of `refund[b]`, the amount the tampered scenario moves, and which hash and builder
   apply. M3's functions are unchanged, and M2's crate compiles the file as before.
   The new binary on M3's batch directory (no `circuit.txt`) reproduced M3's mismatch scenario
   (binding-leaf disprove, 3,134 vB) and its stall scenario.

The check in item 3 is new: M1.md and M3.md left the seller payout scripts unbound ("the
template takes them from the opening's payee labels"). Here a seller signs a script id in the
order, the proof puts the order in `saleCommitment`, and the committee refuses a payee label whose
id differs. The same goes for each buyer's refund script. The fee output pays the sum of
`feeBudget`, and the circuit proves `feeBudget[b] ≤ feeCap[b]` for the cap each buyer signed, so
the fee amount is bounded by signed terms. The fee payee label is not signed by anyone.

`load` in `zkc-m1` also compares the Rust commitments with circomlibjs's
(`batch/sale_commitment.txt`, `escrow_commitment.txt`, written from `statement_signed_tree.js`),
so two implementations must agree before anything runs.

**Not in the template.** Signatures, expiries, `feeBudget ≤ feeCap` and each buyer's equation
`funding = cost + fee + refund` are the proof's job. The tampered statement S′ builds a
template for that reason, as in M3: its refund moves are in neither commitment.

**Unit tests** (18 in `template.rs`, 12 of them new):
- `PoseidonChunked` in Rust equals circomlibjs's at 16 lengths: 1, 2, 8, 11, 12, 13, 18, 20, 23,
  24, 25, 36, 37, 72, 143 and 144 values, with values near r
  (`vectors/chunked-hash-vectors.json`, from [`chunked_vectors.js`](chunked_vectors.js), which
  calls `statement_signed_tree.js`'s `hashChunked`);
- `signed_statement_hash` equals the `statementHash` of M11's own 16×4 and 128×8 statements
  (20 and 36 values) and of the regtest batch, which is also the proof's public signal;
- both commitments equal circomlibjs's for the regtest batch;
- `build_signed` returns 16 seller outputs, 4 refunds and the fee, with the escrows;
- a statement that does not hash to the public input is refused ("does not hash"): S′ against the
  honest hash, each of the 20 values off by one, a wrong hash, and 19 values. S′ under its own
  hash builds a different template;
- `statementHash` + r and each of the 20 statement values + r are refused;
- a changed price or payout id (sale commitment), and a changed fee cap, refund script or escrow id
  (escrow commitment), are refused;
- a payee, refund or escrow label that is not the signed one is refused;
- dust, non-conserving outputs and a wrong `totalPayout` are refused;
- circuit names select the layouts, and malformed names are refused.

The conversion crate adds 4 tests on this verifying key: both proofs verify with one public
input, the hash + 1 and the other proof's hash fail, and hash + r is refused.

**Data availability.** As in M3 only the hash is on chain, so the committee needs the statement
from the operator before presigning, and also the sale list and escrow terms it opens. The
template reads these fields of the circuit input: `price`, `activeSale`, `saleId`, `sellerId`,
`selector`, `buyerId`, `sellerPayoutId`, `activeBuyer`, `escrowId`, `feeCap`, `refundScriptId`.
It does not read the signatures. A challenger needs only what is on chain.

## What M14 does not show

- **The identity-point case** with one input (a hash with a zero first window) was not run. This
  batch's hash ends in byte 188 and the `run.sh` example's in byte 19; M3's hashes end in 237 and
  205.
- **M14 ran on M1's graph.** The M2 figures above are arithmetic on runs of other graphs, not a
  run of the combined graph. M2's crate compiles `template.rs` and passes its tests on it; its
  scenarios were not rerun.
- **The expiry checks mean nothing yet.** `batchHeight` is 905,000 in the statement and the
  regtest chain is at height 200 to 350. The graph does not bind it to the Bitcoin height
  (roadmap item 3).
- **Escrow ids are labels.** The template checks that `escrowId` is the id of the escrow label,
  but the harness funds each escrow with a fresh outpoint, so the outpoint the committee spends is
  not the one a buyer signed. In a real graph the escrow's outpoint has to be hashed into the
  signed id, and the committee has to spend exactly that outpoint.
- **Order keys are not payees' keys.** The BabyJubjub order keys behind `sellerId` and `buyerId`
  are not bound to the sharechain `miner_address` of the share (M5, M10); the regtest payees'
  secp256k1 keys are derived from the labels.
- **The batch has every slot active.** The padding rules (zeroed fields, padding-key signatures)
  ran only in the circuit's R1CS check (`check_padded.sh`), not through BitVM. `build_signed`
  skips an unfunded buyer slot as `build` does, but no test gives it one; M3's `build` tests do
  (their batch has three buyers).
- **The committee is one key**, and the operator, challenger, buyer and seller keys are SHA-256
  of public labels. Winternitz secrets come from a public seed per graph, and the Groth16 setup
  is the insecure deterministic one on a local phase-1 file.
- Everything M1.md and M3.md list as not covered still applies: 362 of 364 disprove leaves need a
