# M9: fee bumping in the optimistic graph, tested in a fee spike

Run 2026-11-01 on Bitcoin Core v31.1.0 (regtest), with the same BitVM build, patch, proof and
demo keys as [M2.md](M2.md). Regtest only: the keys and the Groth16 setup are public.

## Headline

M2's graph fixes every presigned fee at 2 sat/vB. In a fee spike that is a safety failure. When
the market rose to 10 sat/vB, **an honest operator lost its bond on both paths**:

- on the unchallenged path, a payee pinned the payout and then posted the timeout itself;
- on the challenged path, the assert chain could not confirm.

**The anchored graph (M9) settled both paths in the same spike, on the same timeline as at
2 sat/vB.** The operator paid for its own CPFP children. A challenge now pays the operator the
assert chain's cost at the graph's maximum fee rate, 10 sat/vB.

| Run (10 sat/vB market) | M2 graph | Anchored graph |
|---|---|---|
| Unchallenged, a payee pins the payout | The operator's CPFP child is refused (`too-large-cluster`). The payout waits below the market until T_assert, and the pinning payee posts the timeout: 5,000,000 sats burned, 4,989,288 sats to the payee. Buyers refund | The pin is a 1,000 vB TRUC child. The operator's child evicts it (TRUC sibling eviction), and the payout is mined at h_k + 6 with the template's outputs |
| Challenged in the last block of the window | assert_init stays out of every block template for 6 blocks. The timeout slashes the honest operator: 5,000,000 sats burned, 4,995,910 to the slasher | Each of the 7 assert-chain transactions is carried by a child through its keyed anchor. A is confirmed at h_k + 8, 3 blocks before the timeout opens, as at 2 sat/vB. payout_asserted is mined with a child |

What it costs when fees do not move (anchored graph at 2 sat/vB):

| Path (vbytes mined) | M2 | M9 anchored | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honest, unchallenged | 897 | 940 | +43 (the kickoff's anchor) |
| Assert chain (7 transactions) | 422,607 | 422,908 | +301 (7 anchors) |
| Challenge payment to the operator | 848,000 sats | 4,250,000 sats | the chain's cost at 10 sat/vB, children included |

## 1. What changed in the graph

[`m2/src/graph.rs`](m2/src/graph.rs) builds either graph; `ZKC_GRAPH=anchored` selects M9.

| Transaction | Fee fixed when presigned? | M2 | M9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| kickoff | **yes**: every presigned spend names its txid | version 2, no handle | an operator-keyed anchor (330 sats) |
| payout_unchallenged, payout_asserted | yes | version 2. The operator's back output can carry a child, but any payee can pin | **TRUC (version 3).** The back output is the handle; TRUC allows one child of at most 1,000 vB, and a sibling with a higher fee evicts it |
| assert_init, commit[0..4], assert_final | yes | no output can carry a child | an operator-keyed anchor on each (330 sats) |
| challenge, disprove, timeout, refunds | no: the poster sets the fee | at 2 sat/vB in the runs | at the market rate |

Two corrections to M8's table:

- **The kickoff's fee is fixed too.** M8 listed it as "signed when broadcast". Its txid has to
  exist before the committee presigns anything, so the operator cannot re-sign it at another fee.
- **M2's payouts were not unbumpable.** The operator's back output could always carry a child.
  What was missing was protection from pinning, which TRUC adds.

**Why not TRUC everywhere.** The commit transactions are 53–92 kvB, far over TRUC's 10 kvB cap.
Their keyed anchors leave CPFP to the operator alone. Every other output of the assert chain is
spendable only by the operator or by a presigned transaction, so no third party can attach a
descendant.

**Why the payouts need no extra output.** The back output already belongs to the operator. A
TRUC parent may have one unconfirmed child, so a payee's child and the operator's child
compete for that slot, and the operator wins by outbidding.

## 2. The spike runs

`run-m9.sh <work> spike` starts a regtest node with `-blockmintxfee=0.0001` (10 sat/vB). Block
templates and `generatetoaddress` then take only packages paying at least that. Before and after
each child, the scenario asks `getblocktemplate` whether the parent is included. It waits for a
fresh template each time, because Core serves the previous one for several seconds after a
mempool change (the first run read stale templates and was discarded).

### Unchallenged, with a pin

The pinner is the payee with the smallest output: `refund-buyer3`, 43,000 sats. It gains most if
the batch fails. It gets its whole escrow back through the refund, and whoever posts the timeout
takes half the bond.

| Step | M2 graph | Anchored graph |
|---|---|---|
| payout_unchallenged (700 vB, 1,400 sats) alone | not in the template | not in the template |
| The payee's pin | a chain of 111 vB children of its output, 1 sat/vB each. The node accepted 63 and refused the 64th (`too-large-cluster`, Core 31's 64-transaction cluster limit) | one TRUC child, 1,000 vB, paying 15,599 sats: 1 sat less than would carry the payout to 10 sat/vB |
| The operator's child through its back output | refused: `too-large-cluster` | accepted at 15,617 sats; the pin is evicted |
| Outcome | the payout is still unconfirmed when the timeout opens at h_k + 12. The payee's timeout replaces it and its 63 pins. 5,000,000 sats burned, 4,989,288 to the payee; the buyers refund at T_refund | the payout and the child are mined at h_k + 6; the outputs equal the template |

The operator's cost against a TRUC pin is bounded. The pin can be at most 1,000 vB and must stay
below the market with its parent, so evicting it costs at most about the market rate times
1,700 vB: 17,000 sats at 10 sat/vB. The pins against M2's version 2 payout cost the payee
nothing: they were replaced, not mined.

### Challenged in the last block of the window

| Step | M2 graph | Anchored graph |
|---|---|---|
| kickoff | built at the market rate in this run, so it confirms (M2's graph cannot bump it) | 240 vB at 2 sat/vB, out of the template; mined at h_k with a 169 vB child |
| challenge at h_k + 5 | mined | mined |
| assert_init | out of every template for 6 blocks; never confirmed | out of the template alone, in it with a child; mined at h_k + 6 |
| commit[0..4] | — | each out of the template alone and in it with a child; all mined at h_k + 7 |
| assert_final | — | mined with a child at h_k + 8: 3 blocks to spare before T_assert |
| Outcome | the timeout slashes the honest operator at h_k + 12 | payout_asserted is mined with a child at h_A + 6, with the template's outputs. The timeout is rejected afterwards (KA spent) |

In the anchored spike runs the operator paid 3,404,808 sats in child fees: 3,393,972 for the
assert chain, 3,610 for the kickoff and 7,226 for payout_asserted. The assert chain cost
846,938 sats of presigned fees plus 3,393,972 in children, 4,240,910 in all. The challenge paid
4,250,000, so the operator ended 9,090 sats ahead on the assert. The bumps on the kickoff and
the payout are costs it pays on either path.

The challenger's BitVM validation was skipped in the spike runs (`ZKC_VALIDATE=0`). The
fee-rate run below covers it, with the same leaves and assertion.

## 3. The fee-rate runs (no spike)

`run-m9.sh <work> fee` runs M2's six scenarios on the anchored graph at 2 sat/vB, on a node with
default policy. Nothing needs a child, and every scenario ends as it did in M2.

| Scenario | vbytes mined, M2 → M9 | What happened (anchored graph) |
|---|---|---|
| unchallenged | 897 → 940 | payout at h_k + 6 with the template's outputs. The payout one block early and the early timeout are rejected (`non-BIP68-final`); a challenge and a timeout after the payout are rejected (`missing-inputs`) |
| challenged | 423,708 → 424,052 | challenged at h_k + 5. commit[1] beside an unconfirmed assert_init and commit[0] is refused (`too-large-cluster`), as in M2. A is confirmed at h_k + 8, 3 blocks to spare. BitVM validation finds nothing (515 s, 12.5 GB peak). The final-chunk and binding disproves fail consensus. payout_asserted is mined at h_A + 6 |
| tampered | 662,428 → 662,772 | leaf 431 disproves: 238,999 vB, mined by `generateblock`. 5,000,000 sats burned, 4,521,316 to the challenger. The payout and the timeout are rejected afterwards; buyers refund |
| silent | 978 → 1,021 | the timeout slashes at h_k + 12: 5,000,000 burned, 4,998,502 to the poster. Buyers refund |
| incomplete | 423,186 → 423,487 | the assert chain without assert_final; the same timeout slashes, and assert_final is rejected afterwards |
| stall | 390 → 390 | buyers refund after 30 blocks; bond and reserve stay unspent |

Every difference in size is 43 vB per anchor: one on the kickoff and seven on the assert chain.
The slashes pay slightly less than in M2. KB is 416 sats smaller (the kickoff's anchor and its
extra fee), so the timeout pays 416 less. A is 426 sats smaller (assert_final's anchor and extra
fee), so a disprove, which spends both, pays 842 less. Two node runs, each on a fresh chain: unchallenged, silent, incomplete and stall in one;
challenged and tampered in the other.

## 4. What the anchors change in the economics

- **The challenge price.** The challenge's committee signature (SINGLE|ANYONECANPAY) fixes the
  amount paid to the operator. M9 sets it to the assert chain's cost at MAX_FEE_RATE = 10 sat/vB,
  children included: 4,250,000 sats, against M2's 848,000 at 2 sat/vB.
  - An honest operator can then assert through any spike up to 10 sat/vB without losing money.
    The spike run ended 9,090 sats ahead.
  - In a calm market an honest challenged operator ends about 3.4 M sats ahead. A griefing
    challenge costs the griefer about 5 times more than in M2.
  - A fraud-proving challenger pays more up front. Breaking even needs BOND − BURN ≥ the
    challenge plus the disprove, both at the fee rate of the moment. At 10 sat/vB, with the
    largest leaf's disprove (about 990 kvB), that is about 4.25 M + 9.9 M sats: a bond of about
    0.28 BTC with a half burn, *est.* M2 reached the same figure, because it already counted the
    challenge at the fee rate of the moment.
  - Setting MAX_FEE_RATE is choosing the highest fee rate at which the mode is safe. Above it, an
    honest operator who is challenged loses money on the assert, but it can still assert.
- **The bond and burn.** Unchanged from M2: the bond has to cover a fraud proof at the fee rate
  of the moment. M9 does not change the disprove or the timeout. Their posters always set the fee.
- **The TRUC size cap.** A TRUC payout is at most 10,000 vB. M2's payout is about 423 vB plus
  57.5 vB per escrow and 43 vB per payee output. So a batch with 5 escrows fits about 216 payee
  outputs. M7's daily batch (100 miners, 5 market makers) pays at most about 106 distinct outputs,
  about 5.3 kvB, *est.*

## 5. What M9 does not show

- **Rising fees during the assert.** The spike is a constant 10 sat/vB from the kickoff on. A
  market that keeps rising forces the operator to replace its children (RBF on the anchor spend),
  which works the same way and was not run.
- **Relay between peers.** Every transaction was submitted to one node. The TRUC package
  behaviour M8 showed (`submitpackage`) and sibling eviction across peers are not exercised.
- **Pins against the keyed anchors.** Only the operator can spend them, so there is nothing to
  run. A pin through any other output of the assert chain needs a presigned signature.
- **The challenge race and reorgs**, as in M2.
- **Non-standard disproves.** Unchanged: M12 measures them.

## Reproduce

M9 reuses M2's build and M1's caches. In WSL, with `/root/zkc-m1` prepared as in M1.md:

```sh
# A separate work dir that reads M1's batch and cache.
mkdir -p /root/zkc-m9 && ln -s /root/zkc-m1/batch /root/zkc-m9/batch && ln -s /root/zkc-m1/m1 /root/zkc-m9/m1
BITVM_SRC=/root/zkc-m1/BitVM-c620dbf bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/build.sh /root/zkc-m9
BITCOIN_BIN=/root/opt/bitcoin-31.1/bin bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/run-m9.sh /root/zkc-m9 spike
BITCOIN_BIN=/root/opt/bitcoin-31.1/bin bash research/zk_clearing/bitvm/scripts/run-m9.sh /root/zkc-m9 fee
```

[`results/m9/`](results/m9/) holds one trace per scenario and graph, with every txid, size,
mempool verdict, template probe and mined height. [`results/m9/timings.txt`](results/m9/timings.txt)
has the run times.
